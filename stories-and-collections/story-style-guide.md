# Allô Carto — Story Style Guide

## Difficulty

| Difficulty | Words per sentence | Sentences per paragraph | Concepts per sentence |
| ---------- | -----------------: | ----------------------: | --------------------: |
| Very Easy  |                  5 |                       3 |                     1 |
| Easy       |                  7 |                       4 |                     2 |
| Medium     |                  9 |                       5 |                     2 |
| Hard       |                 12 |                       6 |                     3 |
| Very Hard  |                 16 |                       7 |                     4 |
| Master     |                 20 |                       8 |                     5 |

## Grammar

- **Very Easy:** Present tense only, basic words and sentences.
- **Easy:** Negation, conjunctions, near future.
- **Medium:** Past tense, comparisons, connected ideas.
- **Hard:** Imparfait, future tense, relative clauses.
- **Very Hard:** Conditional, subjunctive, complex grammar.
- **Master:** Anything goes.

## New concepts per sentence

New concepts per sentence measures how many **new words or language concepts** a sentence introduces.

A concept can be:

- **Vocabulary:** A new word, like _chat_.
- **Grammar:** A new structure, like _ne...pas_.
- **Expressions:** A phrase learned as a unit, like _avoir faim_.
- **Language rules:** Elision, contractions, word order, etc.
- **Other concepts:** Cultural or language-specific ideas that require explanation.
- **Tips:** Anything else that warrants a tip.

## Rules

- **Each sentence must build on the last using familiar vocabulary and concepts. This is crucial.**
- Establish familiar context → introduce something new → reinforce it.
- Use natural Québec French.
- Dialogue is allowed at Easy and above, but not Very Easy. It's a context
- Higher levels include everything from lower levels.
- Introduce concepts appropriate to the current difficulty.
- Never introduce complexity just to reach new concepts per sentence limit.
- Paragraphs tend to be better when short.

**The goal is to start easy and gradually make the French harder without making the stories feel blahish.**

## Why This Style Guide Exists

When I started writing Allô Carto's stories, I thought short, fun, well written content would be enough to keep users progressing through the app.

I was wrong. Everyone was losing interest by Puzzle 3 of paragraph 2.

I tried different, easier versions of the paragraphs and shorter sentences but nothing really seemed to help, which was frustrating.

Then something interesting happened...by accident. I had rewritten a particularly lengthy paragraph that previous testers had struggled with. But I had forgotten to remove the original and more difficult paragraph.

One of my testers finised the easy one, then immediately started the hard original one right after. She said, **"OHHHH, that's cool! I know this!"** and proceeded to crush every flashcard and all four puzzles that my other testers had a hard time with.

It was super easy for her to learn. I had accidently created a really good recipe to learn pretty much anything.

**Familiar context + narrow focus + new stuff + iterations.**

That's really the core of this style guide. Stories need to create that loop in pretty much every sentence.
