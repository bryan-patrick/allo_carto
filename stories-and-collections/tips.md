# Tips

| ID | Title | Tip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Introducing your name | Je m'appelle is used when introducing your name. The expression literally means I me call (or I call myself). |
| 2 | À with city names | French uses à with city names when saying where someone lives. |
| 3 | Dépanneur in Québec | In Québec, dépanneur means convenience store. |
| 4 | De l'eau | De l'eau is a common French expression meaning some water. You'll see it often when people talk about drinking or having water. |
| 5 | Questions with est-ce que | Est-ce que is a phrase that is placed at the beginning of a statement to turn it into a question. |
| 6 | Dans la ville and en ville | Dans la ville means in the city, but en ville is another common expression that can also mean in the city or urban life. |
| 7 | Negation with ne and pas | Ne and pas both surround a verb to make a statement negative. |
| 8 | Faire for weather and temperature | French uses faire (fait) to describe weather conditions, such as temperature, and how warm or cold a place feels. |
| 9 | Le matin | Le matin is translated more closely to in the morning instead of just the morning. |
| 10 | Le soir | Le soir is translated more closely to in the evening instead of just the evening. |
| 11 | Jusqu'au | Jusqu'au is an expression that means up to or all the way to. It's formed from jusque + à + le. |
| 12 | Faire une sieste | Faire une sieste means to take a nap. French uses faire (do) rather than prendre (take) in this expression. |
| 13 | Du coin | Du coin means local or nearby. It does not necessarily refer to a literal street corner. |
| 14 | Entrer et sortir | Entrer et sortir means to come and go, literally to enter and exit. |
| 15 | Lui before the verb | Lui is an indirect object pronoun meaning to him or to her. It normally appears before the verb. |
| 16 | Pendant que | Pendant que means while, but literally translates to during that. For example, je regarde pendant que je mange means I watch while I eat, but translates to I watch during that I eat. |
